# The pitch script, draft 1 (9 minutes)

**Order:** Rob (the why, 3:00) → Panshul (the numbers, 3:00) → Mi'kael (ESG, then the close, 3:00).
This replaces the older running order that had Rob closing. Written 23 Nov 2026, two days before the pitch.

**How to read it.** Lines starting with ▸ are what builds on screen; they are never spoken. Everything else is spoken. `[PRICE]` and `[VERIFY]` are things to check on pitch morning (list at the bottom). Numbers are from `data/clean` (Bloomberg, 20 Nov) unless marked.

**The story in one line:** *Everyone told them no. The sceptics were wrong, once, and we will show you how to judge whether they are wrong again.* Told from the sceptic's side: the board's doubt is a character in the story, not an obstacle to it.

**Devices used (from the storytelling playbook), so they survive rewriting**
- **Plant and harvest (Chappelle):** a Denny's booth and "nobody starts a company to…" are planted by Rob in the first minute, and harvested by Mi'kael in the last 20 seconds. Rob also plants "watch the screen", which pays off in the reveal.
- **Say the board's doubt out loud first (Carnegie, Duarte's sparkline):** Rob names the scepticism, Panshul says the downside before they can, Mi'kael asks the energy question himself.
- **Escalation in threes, used once:** the four layers, ending on the biggest number.
- **Human story last (Obama, McKee):** the numbers and the controversy come before the cancer screening, so the audience leaves on what AI is *for*.
- **One thread across three voices:** each speaker's last line hands a question to the next (Rob → "is it in the numbers?", Panshul → "who sits in one chair?", Mi'kael → "and the screen").

## ROB: the why (about 3:00)

▸ Black screen. Nothing builds.

Before we start: watch the screen behind me. I'll come back to it.

▸ *Title builds:* 1993. A booth at Denny's.

In 1993, three people sat in a booth at a Denny's in San Jose, drinking a lot of coffee. Jensen Huang, Chris Malachowsky, Curtis Priem. One idea: the 3D graphics on expensive workstations should be in every PC, and video games would pay for it. People told them nobody starts a company to play games.

▸ *Big number builds:* 249,000 of 250,000 chips came back.

Their first chip was a disaster. Of two hundred and fifty thousand made, about two hundred and forty-nine thousand came back. They nearly went under. They survived, invented the GPU, and became a very good gaming company. They could have stopped there.

▸ *Title builds:* 2006. CUDA.

They didn't. In 2006 they made a bet you would have hated. I would have hated it. Here is the idea. A normal processor is a few very fast workers doing one job after another. A graphics chip is thousands of smaller workers doing the same job at once. CUDA let anyone put those thousands of workers to work, and NVIDIA gave it away, inside every card it sold.

Wall Street hated it. Every chip got bigger and more expensive, for gamers who didn't want it. The shares fell by more than half in 2008, and years later an activist fund was still asking why they kept spending. You'd have been sceptical. So was everyone with money.

▸ *Chart builds:* 2012 image contest, error rate: AlexNet 15.3%, runner-up 26.2%.

Then in 2012 a student at the University of Toronto entered an image-recognition contest with a neural network trained on two NVIDIA gaming cards. He didn't win by a little. His error rate was fifteen percent. The runner-up's was twenty-six. And anyone who wanted to copy him needed CUDA. Years of being doubted, paid off in a single contest.

▸ *Four layers build one by one; the last line is the biggest number.*

So why can't anyone catch up? Four layers, and a rival has to beat all four at once. Chips: on paper, AMD's chip has more memory and costs less, yet last year NVIDIA made about twelve dollars from data centres for every dollar AMD made. Software: six million developers write for CUDA. Connections: seventy-two chips wired into one machine. And supply: NVIDIA has reserved about sixty percent of the packaging that assembles them. Four layers. One company.

▸ *Screen clears to a single line:* Is it in the numbers?

That's why it happened. Panshul will show you whether the numbers match the story, and what you would be paying for it.

## PANSHUL: the numbers (about 3:00)

▸ *Chart builds, slowly:* revenue FY22 to FY26, with Data Center shaded.

Rob told you why it happened. I'll show you whether it's in the numbers. It is. Four years ago NVIDIA sold twenty-seven billion dollars. Last year it sold two hundred and sixteen. Eight times, in four years. And ninety percent of it is Data Center: the business that began as a bet nobody wanted.

▸ *Big number:* $107.6bn, one quarter of Data Center, against $27.0bn, all of FY23.

In the quarter to October, Data Center alone sold a hundred and eight billion dollars. That is four times what the whole company sold in the year 2023.

▸ *Two big numbers:* 60% operating margin, $96.7bn free cash flow.

NVIDIA keeps sixty cents of profit from every dollar of sales, and last year ninety-seven billion dollars of cash. Rob's four layers are why. Nobody keeps sixty cents unless the customer can't easily go elsewhere.

▸ *Chart:* consensus revenue FY26 216 → FY27 422 → FY28 729. Small line: beat forecasts 13 quarters in a row.

Analysts expect sales to double again to four hundred and twenty-two billion this year. And NVIDIA has beaten their revenue forecast for thirteen quarters in a row.

▸ *Title builds:* What is a share worth?

So what is a share worth? We built a DCF. Forecast the cash the business will make, then shrink future cash back into today's money at the return investors demand. We used Bloomberg's rate, nineteen percent: a high bar.

▸ *Bars build:* Bear $31 · Base $167.50 · Bull $186 · Today [PRICE] $164.

Our base case says one hundred and sixty-seven dollars fifty. Today's price is about one hundred and sixty-four. So I won't call it a bargain. Last week, before the founder resigned and came back, it was well below our base case. Today it is level with it.

▸ *Two bars:* NVIDIA 17x this year's profit; rivals' median 31x.

Here is the fairer way to say it. Against its rivals it is cheaper, not dearer: about seventeen times this year's profit, against thirty-one for the median of AMD, Intel, Broadcom and TSMC. At this price you are not paying for a miracle. You are paying for analysts to be roughly right.

▸ *Bear bar highlights red.* ▸ *Chart:* build-out as share of US GDP: fibre 1.1%, highways 1.1%, railways 2.2%, AI 3.6%.

Now the risk, said by us first. If AI spending pauses, our bear case is thirty-one dollars, eighty-one percent below today. That's the bubble question. AI is heading for three and a half percent of American GDP every year. The railways took two. The dot-com fibre boom took one. It is the biggest build-out in American history, and that cuts both ways.

▸ *Verdict line builds:* A fair price for the strongest business in its field. [DECISION: wording]

So our verdict: not a bargain at this price, but a fair price for the strongest business in its field, sized for the risk. And notice what the price did last week. About a trillion dollars moved on who sits in one chair. That is Mi'kael's question.

## MI'KAEL: ESG, then the close (about 3:00)

▸ *Title builds:* "AI is an environmental disaster. And you just used it for a slideshow."

Panshul left me the hard question, and I know it's the one you came with. AI is an environmental disaster, and we just used it to build a slideshow. So let me start with the uncomfortable number.

▸ *Chart builds:* NVIDIA total energy FY22–FY26, renewable share filling to 100%.

NVIDIA's own energy use is about one terawatt-hour a year, up seventy-seven percent in two years. All of it is now renewable. Its own direct emissions are small: per million dollars of sales, a twentieth of a tonne, against twenty-five for Intel and a hundred and nine for TSMC.

▸ *Chart, red:* Scope 3, FY24 3.6m tonnes → FY26 10.7m tonnes.

But look at everything outside its own walls: suppliers, customers. Those emissions have nearly tripled in two years, from three point six million tonnes to ten point seven. That is the real footprint, and it grows with the sales. We won't hide it.

▸ *Line builds, plain:* We have not measured this presentation's energy. We won't guess.

As for this slideshow, we haven't measured its energy and we won't invent a number. The test is whether the work is worth the energy.

▸ *Title builds:* Governance.

Then governance. This month the founder resigned. On Monday the board brought him back, unanimously. Whatever you think of that, look at what it did next: a new committee of independent directors for national security and export control, a search for a chief compliance officer, and an independent investigation that carries on. Whether that is enough is your judgement. It is the right question, and it is why our verdict says sized for the risk.

▸ *Three facts build, one at a time. [VERIFY all three]*

But here is why that risk is worth taking. In 2024 the Nobel Prize in Chemistry went to AI work that predicted the shape of almost every known protein. A drug for a lung disease went from idea to clinical trials in under thirty months, less than half the usual time. In Sweden, AI-assisted screening found twenty percent more breast cancers while cutting the doctors' reading work by forty-four percent.

▸ *Screen clears. Pause. The deck's own build speeds up.*

Now, Rob asked you to watch the screen. Every slide you saw was assembled by AI, in time with our voices. In 1993, three people in a booth were told nobody starts a company to play games. Today, three students and one laptop.

Everything you just watched was built and run by AI. The AI industry runs on infrastructure NVIDIA built. If three university students can do this for a stock pitch, imagine what the world's companies are doing.

▸ *Black screen. NVIDIA logo fades in.*

Thank you.

## Decisions for Rob (these change what is said)

1. **The verdict.** At $164.39 our own base case is only about 2% above the price, and the model says a buy only if you think an AI pause is very unlikely (about a 2% chance of the bear case, against about 24% at the 20 Nov price of $135.46). The script therefore says "fair price, not a bargain". If the team wants to recommend a clear Buy, the reason has to be something the numbers don't show (for example, buy on a dip below a stated price). Decide, then edit Panshul's last paragraph.
2. **Scripted versus live.** The reveal says slides were "assembled by AI, in time with our voices", not "unscripted". The main narrative is a scripted spine; only Q&A is truly generated. If a board member asks "was that live?", say so: the content was ours, the AI built it in time with our words, the operator can pause or approve.
3. **Founder-hero risk.** The old script's Beat 14 ("NVIDIA isn't one person", Glassdoor) is cut. The board will now hear any hero story through the Huang news, so the script lets the sceptics be the ones who were wrong about the company, not the founder.
