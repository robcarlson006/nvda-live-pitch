## What the research says about the line between corny and not corny

Read with Obscura (Wikipedia on sentimentality, Paul Graham, Orwell, Literary Hub on David Foster Wallace, MIT Sloan Management Review, Y Combinator's investor-pitch advice), then compared with the writing-quality checklist in your `academic-paper` skill.

1. **Corn is feeling bigger than the evidence.** The literary-criticism definition is "a response is sentimental if it is too great for the occasion" (I. A. Richards, quoted on Wikipedia). Wilde: a sentimentalist wants "the luxury of an emotion without paying for it." Baldwin: "ostentatious parading of excessive and spurious emotion." So the test is not "is this emotional?" but "has the audience been shown enough to earn it?" In a stock pitch the way to pay is with numbers. That is why more numbers and less corn are one edit.
2. **Say it the way you would say it to a friend.** Paul Graham's test: don't let a sentence through unless it is the way you'd say it to a friend; read it aloud and fix what clanks. Fancy phrasing also gives the writer "the false impression that you're saying more than you actually are." Corn is usually a sentence written for a speech, not for a person.
3. **No phrase you have seen in print.** Orwell: never use a figure of speech you are used to seeing in print; if a word can be cut, cut it; short words over long ones. "Years of being doubted, paid off in a single contest" is exactly such a phrase.
4. **Name the trope, then do it sincerely.** David Foster Wallace's *This is Water* works because he says up front that it is a "banal platitude" and that banal platitudes "can have a life or death importance" (Literary Hub's reading). The same move works for our one planned sentimental moment, the Denny's callback: we say we noticed it, and let the audience smile.
5. **Say what you are doing first; boilerplate is ignored.** Y Combinator's advice to founders pitching investors: say what you're doing in the first sentence; a vague description ("collaboratively leverage the value of information") goes in one ear and out the other; keep secondary matters short; speak slowly. So the pitch now states its answer in the first line, and story plus numbers carry it (MIT Sloan's framing: a data story is a narrative, visuals and a takeaway; the Kahneman line "no one ever made a decision because of a number, they need a story" is quoted second-hand there, so it is not used in the script).
6. **Checklist applied (from your `academic-paper` writing-quality reference).** Cut throat-clearing; cut slogan cadence and rule-of-three pairs ("Four layers. One company."); cut "not X but Y" contrasts; one precise word, not a grand one ("strongest business in its field" became a number).

**The rule for this team in one line:** every feeling is next to a number the screen can show; if a sentence has no number and no plain fact, ask what it is for.

## ROB: the why (about 2:35, 334 words)

▸ Black screen. Then one line builds: **NVIDIA: a fair price for the best business in AI.**

Here is our answer first. NVIDIA is the best business in AI, the share price is about fair, and you should know exactly what risk comes with it. We'll show you the numbers, including the ones that argue against us. One thing: watch the screen. We'll explain it at the end.

▸ *Title builds:* 1993. A Denny's in San Jose.

It starts in 1993, in a Denny's in San Jose. Jensen Huang, Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem wanted to put workstation graphics in every PC, and video games would pay for it. People told them nobody starts a company to play games.

▸ *Big number:* 249,000 of 250,000.

Their first chip failed. Of two hundred and fifty thousand made, two hundred and forty-nine thousand came back.

▸ *Line chart builds:* revenue FY07 $3.1bn → FY16 $5.0bn, with "CUDA, 2006" marked.

Then in 2006 they made a bet that looked like a mistake. A normal processor is a few fast workers doing one job after another. A graphics chip is thousands of slower workers doing the same job at once. CUDA let anyone program those thousands of workers, and NVIDIA gave it away inside every card, which made every chip dearer for gamers who never asked for it. The year CUDA launched, revenue was three billion dollars. Nine years later it was five. If you doubted it then, the numbers were on your side.

▸ *Chart builds:* 2012 image contest, error rate: 15.3% v 26.2%. ▸ *Line chart continues to FY26: $5.0bn → $215.9bn.*

In 2012 a student in Toronto trained a neural network on two NVIDIA gaming cards and entered an image-recognition contest. His error rate was fifteen percent. The runner-up's was twenty-six. Anyone who wanted to copy him needed CUDA. Revenue went from five billion in 2016 to two hundred and sixteen billion last year.

▸ *Four numbers build one by one:* 11.6× AMD's data-centre sales · 6 million developers · 72 chips in one machine · ~60% of packaging.

Why can't rivals catch up? They have to beat four things at once. On chips, AMD's has more memory and costs less, yet NVIDIA's data-centre sales are twelve times AMD's. On software, six million developers write for CUDA. On networking, NVIDIA links seventy-two chips into one machine. And it has booked about sixty percent of the packaging that assembles them.

▸ *Screen clears to one line:* Do the numbers agree?

That's the why. Panshul will show you whether the numbers agree, and what you'd pay for it.

## PANSHUL: the numbers (about 3:20, 431 words)

▸ *Chart builds, slowly:* revenue FY22 $26.9bn → FY26 $215.9bn, Data Center shaded.

Do the numbers agree? Yes. Four years ago NVIDIA sold twenty-seven billion dollars. Last year it sold two hundred and sixteen: eight times as much. Ninety percent of that is Data Center. In the latest quarter alone, Data Center sold a hundred and eight billion dollars, four times the whole company's sales in 2023.

▸ *Bars:* operating margin NVIDIA 60% · AMD 29% (consensus) · Intel 15% (consensus). ▸ *Big number:* $96.7bn free cash flow.

NVIDIA keeps sixty cents of profit from every dollar of sales. Analysts expect AMD to keep twenty-nine and Intel fifteen. Last year that left NVIDIA with ninety-seven billion dollars of free cash.

▸ *Chart:* consensus revenue FY26 $216bn → FY27 $422bn (+96%) → FY28 $729bn (+73%). ▸ *Small panel:* Q3 FY27 revenue $115.8bn v $109.3bn expected; profit per share $2.68 v $2.47.

Analysts expect sales of four hundred and twenty-two billion this year, up ninety-six percent, and seven hundred and twenty-nine billion next year, up seventy-three. They've been too low for thirteen quarters running. Last quarter NVIDIA reported a hundred and sixteen billion against a hundred and nine forecast, and profit per share of two sixty-eight against two forty-seven.

▸ *Title builds:* What is one share worth?

So what is one share worth? We built a discounted cash flow. We forecast the cash NVIDIA will make over five years, add everything after that, and shrink it all back into today's money at the return investors demand. We used Bloomberg's rate, nineteen percent, which is a high bar.

▸ *Bars build:* Bear $31.40 · Base $167.50 · Bull $185.74 · Today $164.39.

Our base case is a hundred and sixty-seven dollars fifty. Today's price is a hundred and sixty-four thirty-nine. That is a fair price, not a cheap one, and we would rather say so than pretend.

▸ *Small table:* 56% of value comes after 2032 · discount rate 12% → $324 · 19.3% → $167.50.

Here is what that answer rests on. Fifty-six percent of the value comes from years after 2032. And it moves with the rate: at twelve percent the same cash is worth three hundred and twenty-four dollars. We picked the strict one.

▸ *Number builds:* the price already assumes 38% a year → about $2 trillion of sales by 2032.

Turn it around. At today's price the market already assumes sales grow thirty-eight percent a year for five years. That is about two trillion dollars of sales in 2032. That's the bet in the price.

▸ *Two bars:* forward P/E NVIDIA 17× · rivals' median 31×. ▸ *Small line:* expected profit growth NVIDIA 72% · AMD 120% · Broadcom 65% · TSMC 34% · Intel 19%.

Against rivals, NVIDIA costs seventeen times this year's expected profit. The median of AMD, Intel, Broadcom and TSMC is thirty-one. And its profit is expected to grow faster than all of theirs except AMD's.

▸ *Bear bar goes red.* ▸ *Chart:* build-out as a share of US GDP: fibre 1.1% · highways 1.1% · railways 2.2% · AI 3.6%.

Now the risk, from us first. If AI spending pauses, our bear case is thirty-one dollars, eighty-one percent below today's price. AI is on track to cost three point six percent of American GDP a year. The railways took two point two. The dot-com fibre boom took one point one. It's the biggest build-out ever, and that cuts both ways.

▸ *Line builds:* **+30% on the day the founder returned.**

And look at what the price did on Monday. It rose about thirty percent the day the founder was reinstated. A lot of value sits in one person's chair. Mi'kael will take that.

## MI'KAEL: ESG, then the close (about 2:50, 370 words)

▸ *Title builds:* "AI is an environmental disaster. And you just used it for a slideshow."

Panshul left me the question you came with. AI is an environmental disaster, and we used it to build a slideshow. So here are the numbers we least want to show you.

▸ *Chart builds:* NVIDIA energy use FY22–FY26, 425 → 1,053 thousand MWh; renewable share to 100%.

NVIDIA used about one terawatt-hour of energy last year, up seventy-seven percent in two years. All of it was renewable. Its own emissions are small: a twentieth of a tonne per million dollars of sales, against twenty-five for Intel and a hundred and nine for TSMC.

▸ *Chart, red:* Scope 3 emissions FY24 3.6m tonnes → FY26 10.7m tonnes.

But emissions outside its own walls, from suppliers and customers, went from three point six million tonnes to ten point seven in two years. That's the real footprint, and it grows with sales. We haven't measured this presentation's energy and won't invent a number for it. The question is whether the work is worth the energy.

▸ *Title builds:* Governance.

Governance next. Earlier this month the founder resigned. On Monday the board reinstated him, unanimously. In between it created a committee of independent directors for national security and export control, started a search for a chief compliance officer, and kept its investigation going. He owns about three and a half percent of the company. Ten of the eleven directors are independent [VERIFY]. Whether that is enough is your judgement. It is the reason our verdict says "fair", not "cheap".

▸ *Three numbers build:* 30 months idea → trials · +20% cancers found · −44% reading work. *[VERIFY all]*

Here is what the energy buys. In 2024 the Nobel Prize in Chemistry went to AI work that predicted the shape of almost every known protein. A drug for a lung disease went from idea to clinical trials in under thirty months, less than half the usual time. In Sweden, AI-assisted breast screening found twenty percent more cancers and cut doctors' reading work by forty-four percent.

▸ *Screen clears. Pause.*

Rob asked you to watch the screen. Every slide you saw was built by AI, in time with our voices. In 1993, three people in a booth had an idea nobody believed in. We did notice that we're three people with one laptop. We'll leave you to decide how far to take that.

Everything you just watched was built and run by AI. The AI industry runs on infrastructure NVIDIA built. If three university students can do this for a stock pitch, imagine what the world's companies are doing.

▸ *Black screen. NVIDIA logo fades in.*

Thank you.
