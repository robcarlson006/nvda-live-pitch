## ROB: the why (about 2:55)

▸ Black screen. One line builds: **NVIDIA: the hardest position in AI to copy.**

Here is our answer first. NVIDIA holds the hardest position in AI to copy, and today's share price sits at the low end of what we think it is worth. We'll show you how low, and what could prove us wrong. One thing: watch the screen. We'll explain it at the end.

▸ *Title builds:* 1993. A Denny's in San Jose.

It starts in 1993, in a Denny's in San Jose. Jensen Huang, Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem wanted to put workstation graphics in every PC, and video games would pay for it. People told them nobody starts a company to play games.

▸ *Big number:* 249,000 of 250,000.

Their first chip failed. Of two hundred and fifty thousand made, two hundred and forty-nine thousand came back. Six years later, in 1999, they launched the GeForce 256 and called it the world's first GPU. They had invented the graphics processing unit.

▸ *Title builds:* November 2006. CUDA. ▸ *Line chart builds:* revenue FY07 $3.1bn → FY16 $5.0bn.

Then, in November 2006, they made a bet that looked like a mistake. A normal processor is a few fast workers doing one job after another. A graphics chip is thousands of slower workers doing the same job at once. CUDA, NVIDIA's software, let anyone program those thousands of workers. NVIDIA gave it away inside every card, which made every chip dearer for gamers who never asked for it. The year CUDA launched, revenue was three billion dollars. Nine years later it was five.

▸ *Chart builds:* 2012 image contest, error rate 15.3% v 26.2%. ▸ *Line chart continues to FY26: $5.0bn → $215.9bn.*

In 2012 a student in Toronto trained a neural network on two NVIDIA gaming cards and entered an image-recognition contest. His error rate was fifteen percent. The runner-up's was twenty-six. Anyone who wanted to copy him needed CUDA. Revenue went from five billion dollars in 2016 to two hundred and sixteen billion last year.

▸ *Big number builds:* **20 years** of CUDA. Then beneath it: 1,000+ libraries · 6 million developers.

That is the moat, and it is twenty years deep. CUDA is twenty years old this month. It has over a thousand libraries built on it and six million developers writing for it. A rival can copy a chip. It cannot copy twenty years of software.

▸ *Four lines build:* Chips · Software · Networking · Supply, with 11.6× AMD's data-centre sales · 6 million developers · 72 chips in one machine · ~60% of packaging.

And the software sits on the best of everything else: data-centre sales twelve times AMD's, seventy-two chips wired into one machine, and about sixty percent of TSMC's advanced packaging booked. A rival has to match all of it at once.

▸ *Two bars:* share of AI chip supply, Q4 2025: NVIDIA 68% · Google 19%.

Only one company has come close: Google. It started building its own AI chip in 2015, over a decade ago, with all of Google's cash. Even so, Google has nineteen percent of AI chip supply and NVIDIA has sixty-eight. Almost nobody has the cash or the years.

▸ *Screen clears to one line:* Do the numbers agree?

That's the why. Panshul will show you whether the numbers agree, and what you'd pay for it.

## PANSHUL: the numbers (about 2:50)

▸ *Chart builds, slowly:* revenue FY22 $26.9bn → FY26 $215.9bn, Data Center shaded.

Do the numbers agree? Yes. Four years ago NVIDIA sold twenty-seven billion dollars. Last year it sold two hundred and sixteen: eight times as much. Ninety percent of that is Data Center. In the latest quarter alone, Data Center sold a hundred and eight billion dollars, four times the whole company's sales in 2023.

▸ *Bars:* operating margin NVIDIA 60% · AMD 29% (consensus) · Intel 15% (consensus). ▸ *Big number:* $96.7bn free cash flow.

NVIDIA keeps sixty cents of profit from every dollar of sales. Analysts expect AMD to keep twenty-nine and Intel fifteen. Last year that left NVIDIA with ninety-seven billion dollars of free cash.

▸ *Chart:* consensus revenue FY26 $216bn → FY27 $422bn (+96%) → FY28 $729bn (+73%). ▸ *Small panel:* Q3 FY27 revenue $115.8bn v $109.3bn expected; profit per share $2.68 v $2.47.

Analysts expect sales of four hundred and twenty-two billion this year, up ninety-six percent, and seven hundred and twenty-nine billion next year, up seventy-three. Last quarter NVIDIA reported a hundred and sixteen billion against a hundred and nine forecast.

▸ *Title builds:* What is one share worth? ▸ *Two rates appear:* Bloomberg 19.3% · US semiconductor average 10.55%.

So what is one share worth? We built a discounted cash flow. We forecast the cash NVIDIA will make over five years, add everything after that, and shrink it all back into today's money at the return investors demand. We show two rates. Bloomberg's is nineteen percent, a very high bar. The second is the average for American semiconductor companies, which is ten and a half.

▸ *Bars build:* Bear $31 · Base $167.50 · **Base at 10.55%: $391** · Bull $186 · Today $164.39.

At Bloomberg's rate our base case is a hundred and sixty-seven dollars fifty. Today's price is a hundred and sixty-four. At the industry rate the same cash is worth three hundred and ninety-one dollars, more than double. So today's price is the low end of our range. It only looks fair if you demand Bloomberg's very high return.

▸ *Number builds:* at Bloomberg's rate, the price already assumes 38% a year → about $2 trillion of sales by 2032.

Turn it around. At Bloomberg's rate, today's price already assumes sales grow thirty-eight percent a year for five years. That is about two trillion dollars of sales in 2032.

▸ *Bear bars, held on screen:* $31 (−81%) at 19.3% · $64 (−61%) at 10.55%.

Now the risk, from us first. Everything here depends on AI spending continuing. If it pauses, our bear case is thirty-one dollars at Bloomberg's rate, eighty-one percent below today, and sixty-four dollars at the industry rate, sixty-one percent below. That is the downside, and we are showing it to you. Mi'kael will tell you why we think it is very unlikely.

In 2025, AI systems from Google DeepMind and OpenAI reached gold-medal level at the International Mathematical Olympiad. Google's AlphaEvolve was given fifty open maths problems. It matched the best known answer on three-quarters of them, and beat it on one in five. In May this year an OpenAI model disproved a conjecture in discrete geometry that mathematicians had worked on for eighty years [VERIFY].

Then governance. Earlier this month the founder resigned. On Monday the board reinstated him, unanimously. ## Decisions and flags

1. **The Xi and Trump quotes.** You asked for both saying that whoever wins the AI race becomes the dominant superpower, with military supremacy. I could not find that wording from either man. What I found and used:
   - **White House, 23 Jul 2025** (press release for the AI Action Plan): David Sacks, the AI and crypto adviser, said "To remain the leading economic and military power, the United States must win the AI race." That is Sacks, not Trump, so the script says "its own AI adviser".
   - **Trump, same day:** reported (NPR) saying America will win the AI race. I did not read his full transcript, so I have not quoted him. If you want a direct Trump line, open the [Tech Policy Press transcript](https://techpolicy.press/transcript-donald-trumps-address-at-winning-the-ai-race-event) and pick one you can see on the page.
   - **Xi, April 2025** (Politburo study session): self-reliance in AI and closing the gap with the US (Reuters, CSET translation). Not a "dominant power" line. The script paraphrases it.
   - **Putin, 2017**: "Whoever becomes the leader in this sphere will become the ruler of the world." This is the one well-documented line that says what you wanted. Cut it if you only want Xi and Trump.
   Do not put words in Xi's or Trump's mouths on a slide unless you have read them on the page.
2. **"First GPU" is NVIDIA's own claim.** The GeForce 256 was marketed as "the world's first GPU", a term NVIDIA defined itself. The script says "called it the world's first GPU", which is true.
3. **The closing is now "And none of it happens without NVIDIA."** True of the AI industry (GPUs and CUDA made modern AI possible, as Rob's 2012 story shows). Not a claim about what trained the model that builds our slides, which we have not verified. If asked, say "the industry", not "this model".
4. **The bear case is now the hinge of the whole pitch.** Panshul shows it, Mi'kael answers it. If a board member asks "what if spending stops?", the answer is the two reasons above.
5. **I did not find a verified flood-forecasting source,** so it is not in. The maths, proteins, antibiotic, cancer screening and weather items are.
