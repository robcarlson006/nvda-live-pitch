## ROB: the why (about 3:10, 441 words)

▸ Black screen. One line builds: **NVIDIA: the hardest position in AI to copy.**

Here is our answer first. NVIDIA holds the hardest position in AI to copy, and today's share price sits at the low end of what we think it is worth. We'll show you how low, and what could prove us wrong. One thing: watch the screen. We'll explain it at the end.

▸ *Title builds:* 1993. A Denny's in San Jose.

It starts in 1993, in a Denny's in San Jose. Jensen Huang, Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem wanted to put workstation graphics in every PC, and video games would pay for it. People told them nobody starts a company to play games.

▸ *Big number:* 249,000 of 250,000.

Their first chip failed. Of two hundred and fifty thousand made, two hundred and forty-nine thousand came back.

▸ *Title builds:* November 2006. CUDA. ▸ *Line chart builds:* revenue FY07 $3.1bn → FY16 $5.0bn.

Then, in November 2006, they made a bet that looked like a mistake. A normal processor is a few fast workers doing one job after another. A graphics chip is thousands of slower workers doing the same job at once. CUDA, NVIDIA's software, let anyone program those thousands of workers. NVIDIA gave it away inside every card, which made every chip dearer for gamers who never asked for it. The year CUDA launched, revenue was three billion dollars. Nine years later it was five. If you doubted it then, the numbers were on your side.

▸ *Chart builds:* 2012 image contest, error rate 15.3% v 26.2%. ▸ *Line chart continues to FY26: $5.0bn → $215.9bn.*

In 2012 a student in Toronto trained a neural network on two NVIDIA gaming cards and entered an image-recognition contest. His error rate was fifteen percent. The runner-up's was twenty-six. Anyone who wanted to copy him needed CUDA. Revenue went from five billion dollars in 2016 to two hundred and sixteen billion last year.

▸ *Big number builds:* **20 years** of CUDA. Then beneath it: 1,000+ libraries · 6 million developers.

That is the moat, and it is twenty years deep. CUDA is twenty years old this month. It has over a thousand libraries built on it and six million developers writing for it. A rival can copy a chip. It cannot copy twenty years of software.

▸ *Four lines build:* Chips · Software · Networking · Supply, with 11.6× AMD's data-centre sales · 6 million developers · 72 chips in one machine · ~60% of packaging.

And the software sits on top of the best of everything else. NVIDIA's data-centre sales are twelve times AMD's. It wires seventy-two chips into one machine. And it has booked about sixty percent of the advanced packaging at TSMC. A rival has to match all of it at once.

▸ *Two bars:* share of AI chip supply, Q4 2025: NVIDIA 68% · Google 19%. ▸ *Beneath:* Google's chip programme: 2015 → 2026 · Alphabet's capex 2026: about $200bn.

Only one company has come close: Google. It started building its own AI chip in 2015, so it has had over a decade, and it will spend about two hundred billion dollars on infrastructure this year. Even so, Google has nineteen percent of AI chip supply and NVIDIA has sixty-eight. To come close you need the cash of Google and more than ten years. Almost nobody has either.

▸ *Screen clears to one line:* Do the numbers agree?

That's the why. Panshul will show you whether the numbers agree, and what you'd pay for it.

## PANSHUL: the numbers (about 3:10, 424 words)

▸ *Chart builds, slowly:* revenue FY22 $26.9bn → FY26 $215.9bn, Data Center shaded.

Do the numbers agree? Yes. Four years ago NVIDIA sold twenty-seven billion dollars. Last year it sold two hundred and sixteen: eight times as much. Ninety percent of that is Data Center. In the latest quarter alone, Data Center sold a hundred and eight billion dollars, four times the whole company's sales in 2023.

▸ *Bars:* operating margin NVIDIA 60% · AMD 29% (consensus) · Intel 15% (consensus). ▸ *Big number:* $96.7bn free cash flow.

NVIDIA keeps sixty cents of profit from every dollar of sales. Analysts expect AMD to keep twenty-nine and Intel fifteen. Last year that left NVIDIA with ninety-seven billion dollars of free cash.

▸ *Chart:* consensus revenue FY26 $216bn → FY27 $422bn (+96%) → FY28 $729bn (+73%). ▸ *Small panel:* Q3 FY27 revenue $115.8bn v $109.3bn expected; profit per share $2.68 v $2.47.

Analysts expect sales of four hundred and twenty-two billion this year, up ninety-six percent, and seven hundred and twenty-nine billion next year, up seventy-three. They've been too low for thirteen quarters running. Last quarter NVIDIA reported a hundred and sixteen billion against a hundred and nine forecast.

▸ *Title builds:* What is one share worth? ▸ *Two rates appear:* Bloomberg 19.3% · US semiconductor average 10.55%.

So what is one share worth? We built a discounted cash flow. We forecast the cash NVIDIA will make over five years, add everything after that, and shrink it all back into today's money at the return investors demand. We show two rates. Bloomberg's is nineteen percent, a very high bar. The second is the average for American semiconductor companies, which is ten and a half.

▸ *Bars build:* Bear $31 · Base $167.50 · **Base at 10.55%: $391** · Bull $186 · Today $164.39.

At Bloomberg's rate our base case is a hundred and sixty-seven dollars fifty. Today's price is a hundred and sixty-four. At the industry rate the same cash is worth three hundred and ninety-one dollars, more than double. So today's price is the low end of our range. It only looks fair if you demand Bloomberg's very high return.

▸ *Number builds:* at Bloomberg's rate, the price already assumes 38% a year → about $2 trillion of sales by 2032.

Turn it around. At Bloomberg's rate, today's price already assumes sales grow thirty-eight percent a year for five years. That is about two trillion dollars of sales in 2032.

▸ *Two bars:* forward P/E NVIDIA 17× · rivals' median 31×. ▸ *Small line:* expected profit growth NVIDIA 72% · AMD 120% · Broadcom 65% · TSMC 34% · Intel 19%.

Against rivals, NVIDIA costs seventeen times this year's expected profit. The median of AMD, Intel, Broadcom and TSMC is thirty-one. Its profit is expected to grow faster than all of theirs except AMD's.

▸ *Bear bars:* $31 (−81%) at 19.3% · $64 (−61%) at 10.55%. ▸ *Chart:* build-out as a share of US GDP: fibre 1.1% · highways 1.1% · railways 2.2% · AI 3.6%.

Now the risk, from us first. If AI spending pauses, our bear case is thirty-one dollars at Bloomberg's rate, eighty-one percent below today, and sixty-four dollars at the industry rate, sixty-one percent below. AI is on track to cost three point six percent of American GDP a year. The railways took two point two. The dot-com fibre boom took one point one. It's the biggest build-out ever, and that cuts both ways.

▸ *Line builds:* **+30% on the day the founder returned.**

And look at what the price did on Monday. It rose about thirty percent the day the founder was reinstated. A lot of value sits in one person's chair. Mi'kael will take that.

## MI'KAEL: ESG, what AI has done, then the close (about 3:05, 402 words)

▸ *Title builds:* Is AI an environmental disaster?

Panshul left me the question you came with. Is AI an environmental disaster? Here are the numbers for NVIDIA's own operations.

▸ *Bar chart builds, one bar at a time (log scale, labels on every bar):* own emissions per $1m of sales: NVIDIA 0.05 · AMD 1.2 · Broadcom 2.8 · Intel 25 · TSMC 109 tonnes.

NVIDIA's own operations emitted about ten thousand tonnes of carbon dioxide last year. For every million dollars of sales, that is a twentieth of a tonne. AMD emits one point two. Broadcom, two point eight. Intel, twenty-five. TSMC, a hundred and nine. AMD and Broadcom also design chips, and they emit twenty-six and fifty-nine times more per dollar of sales. All the electricity NVIDIA uses is renewable.

▸ *Title builds:* Governance.

Then governance. Earlier this month the founder resigned. On Monday the board reinstated him, unanimously. It created a committee of independent directors for national security and export control, started a search for a chief compliance officer, and kept its investigation going. He owns about three and a half percent. Ten of eleven directors are independent [VERIFY]. Whether that is enough is your judgement. It is why we show you two rates and a bear case.

▸ *Five items build, one at a time; the last holds.* **170,000 → 200 million proteins · 3 days: new antibiotic · <30 months: idea to trials · +20% cancers found, −44% reading · beat NOAA's hurricane model.** *[VERIFY all]*

Here is what the energy goes into. For sixty years, scientists mapped about a hundred and seventy thousand protein structures. In 2022, AI predicted around two hundred million, nearly every known protein, and won the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. In 2019, AI found a new antibiotic that works on drug-resistant bacteria in three days. A drug for a lung disease went from idea to clinical trials in under thirty months. In Sweden, AI breast screening found at least twenty percent more cancers and cut doctors' reading work by forty-four percent. And in 2025 an AI weather model beat the US government's own on hurricane tracks.

▸ *Screen clears. Pause.*

Rob asked you to watch the screen. Every slide you saw was built live, by AI, as we spoke.

▸ *One line builds, plain:* We have not measured this presentation's energy. We won't guess.

So yes: we used AI to build a slideshow about whether AI is worth its energy. We haven't measured what this one used, and we won't invent a number. The test is the same as for everything else. Is the work worth it?

In 1993, three people in a booth had an idea nobody believed in. We did notice that we're three people with one laptop. We'll leave you to decide how far to take that.

Everything you just watched was built and run by AI. The AI industry runs on infrastructure NVIDIA built. If three university students can do this for a stock pitch, imagine what the world's companies are doing.

▸ *Black screen. NVIDIA logo fades in.*

Thank you.

## Decisions that need you

1. **"Trained and running on NVIDIA hardware" (you asked for this in the reveal).** I kept the closing line as your project brief words it: "The AI industry runs on infrastructure NVIDIA built." Your own `CLAUDE.md` says not to claim the AI in the app runs exclusively on NVIDIA hardware, and I think that is right: the model that builds the slides was not trained only on NVIDIA chips (AI labs use a mix, including Google's and Amazon's). A board member who knows that would catch it, in a pitch about trust. If you want a stronger line that stays true, say what you can prove: "built by AI, running in data centres full of NVIDIA chips" is true of most of the industry. Tell me which wording you want.
2. **Google "isn't taking much share": mostly true, but say it with the number.** Epoch AI puts Google at about 19% of AI chip supply against NVIDIA's 68% (Q4 2025); IDC puts NVIDIA near 81%. That is a big gap. But the trend is real and the board may know it: Anthropic has committed to up to a million of Google's chips; Meta is reported to be in talks; and JPMorgan's revised note counts NVIDIA at only about 28% of *announced future* frontier-lab capacity, with Google's chips at 14% and Broadcom-built custom chips at 23%. The script uses only the present-tense number. Prepare the rest for Q&A (below).
3. **Leaving out Scope 3.** NVIDIA's emissions from outside its own operations, mainly suppliers and customers, rose from 3.6 to 10.7 million tonnes in two years. The new section leaves them out as you asked, and says "NVIDIA's own operations" so every sentence is true. A sceptical ESG board is likely to ask. Prepare the answer (below) even if the script doesn't say it.
4. **The verdict has moved.** v1 and v2 said "fair, not cheap". With two rates the honest line is "the price is the low end of our range": fair at the strictest rate, more than double at the industry average. That is a stronger pitch and still honest, because both are on screen and the bear case is shown at both.
